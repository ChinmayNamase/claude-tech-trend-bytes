# House style — Tech Trend Bytes

Taken from Chinmay's own posts. Follow it so articles sound like him, not like AI.

## Voice
- Short paragraphs: 1–3 sentences, about 17 words each. Never over 60 words.
- Plain words. If a simpler word exists, use it.
- Have an opinion, and say who something is for: "buy this if…", "wait if…".
- Speak to one reader as "you".
- Never bluff. If something is not confirmed, say so and say who reported it.

## Structure
1. Two or three short paragraphs saying what happened and why it matters
2. `## The Short Version` — 4–6 bullets a reader can act on
3. Five to eight `##` sections, each with a clear, plain heading
4. `## Quick Answers` — 3–4 real questions with two-line answers
5. `## The Bottom Line` — the verdict, plus what is still unknown

## India first
- Prices in rupees, always. Add the launch price and today's street price when comparing.
- Say where a phone is sold (Flipkart, Amazon, brand site) and name the bank offers.
- Indian model names differ from global ones — check before listing a device.

## Facts and sources
- Two independent sources for any number. Link the source to the sentence it supports.
- Label leaks: "reportedly", "not confirmed", "expected".
- Never cite YouTube videos or a site that only repeats another site.
- Trusted: 91mobiles, Gadgets 360, Digit, Beebom, GSMArena, Smartprix, BusinessToday, Samsung Newsroom, Google blog, Flipkart and Amazon's own pages.

## Never write
- "In today's fast-paced world", "delve into", "game-changer", "unleash", "unlock the power"
- Invented statistics, fake quotes, or prices no source has published
- Headlines promising something the article does not deliver
